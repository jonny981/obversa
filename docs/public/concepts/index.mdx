Obversa is an environment for software factories.

You do not get one fixed factory. You get the tools to build your own
production lines. Your factory runs in your terminal, with your multiplexer,
and with the agents you already pay for.

## The model in five sentences

An **Order** is the product. It is the change that you want.

A **production line** takes the Order through stations until the work is
complete.

A **callback gate** stops the line until an answerer supplies a structured
result. The answerer can be you, an agent, or a remote service.

**Evidence** shows what the factory did and why the result is correct.

**Lines** is the runtime below all of this. It runs the graphs, records the
events, and keeps the proof.

## What Obversa is not

Obversa is not an editor. Your editor stays.

Obversa is not a terminal or a multiplexer. Obversa is a guest in the host
that you selected.

Obversa is not one factory recipe. You design the lines. The recipes in the
bank are examples, not rules.

Obversa is not a hosted service. Your factory runs on your computer, on your
repositories, with your credentials.

## Why this shape

Physical factories standardize their products because tools such as dies and
presses have a high cost. Software has no such tools. Thus an Order can have the
shape that you want, and a line can flow one Order at a time.

You already have an editor, a terminal, and agents. Obversa does not replace
them. Obversa adds the layer that they do not have: the line, the gates, and
the proof.
