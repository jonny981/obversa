Move the one step that needs a bigger machine, and keep everything else
where you can read it. Most steps in a run are cheap. One or two aren't:
the image build, the suite that takes an hour, the step that needs more
than the machine on your desk.

A remote node will move one of those steps and nothing else. The step
executes on another machine; the runtime stays where you started it, and
the event record is written where it always was. The change comes back as
a commit or a patch, not as a filesystem to keep in sync.

## What stays local

- **The graph and its decisions**, recorded locally, so the record stays
  one file on one machine.
- **The approvals.** A person answers where the person is.
- **The change**, as a commit: atomic, with its own description, reviewable
  without the machine that made it.

## Limits

- **It won't distribute a run.** One named step is given a place to
  execute; steps don't migrate for load.
- **An unreachable machine fails the step where it stands**, with the
  reason on the record.
